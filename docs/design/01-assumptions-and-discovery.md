# 01. Assumptions and Critical Discovery Questions

This document lists what we do not yet know, what we have assumed so design
can proceed, and the documents we need before any real analyzer driver is
written. Labels follow [docs/README.md](../README.md).

assumption and every document that cites it.

RS-232 instruments are reached through a **serial device server** (RS-232 to TCP, raw socket mode) placed within 15 m of the analyzer. | If a physical server with COM ports is used, the serial transport is configured instead. Both transports are designed. | D-13, D-17, D-23 |
| A-2 | Target CPU architecture is **x86-64**. | Native serial module prebuilds and the bundled Node.js must be rebuilt for ARM64. | D-17 |

| A-4 | The middleware is the **source of truth for analyzer orders**. Analyzer queries are answered from the local database only, never by a synchronous HMIS call, so a reply always meets the analyzer's timeout. | None expected. | n/a |

| A-6 | Results are released to the HMIS **only after a person releases them**. Autoverification is off and can only be enabled per test after documented laboratory validation; when it is on it approves, and a person still releases (A-19). | n/a (policy) | Lab director |
| A-37 | **The call target time comes only from the lab.** An approver sets, with their password and the lab policy it comes from, how many minutes (1 to 1440) after a critical version arrives the call must be recorded: one lab-wide target and, optionally, one per test, which wins for that test. Setting a target for the same test again retires the old one; a target is never edited, and every change is audited. The target in force when the page is shown is used, so a change applies to calls already waiting. With no target set, nothing is shown as late. Past the target, the Critical page, the result page and the dashboard say so; nobody else is told and approval is not blocked any further. Targets by priority, ward or time of day are not built. | Lab director to give the target (for example from the lab's critical-result policy) and whether it differs by test, priority or ward; who is told when it passes (escalation steps), which needs the SMS or e-mail gateway (13, phase 5). | Lab director, D-27 |
| A-38 | **R-4s uses a run window the lab sets.** Instruments send each control as its own QC message with no run identifier, so for a range with R-4s the lab gives a window of 1 to 240 minutes: the other controls of the same test on the same instrument (other QC ranges, so other levels or lots) checked within that many minutes before this one are its run. R-4s is broken when this control is more than 2 SD from its mean on one side and one of those is more than 2 SD on the other side, each against its own range's target mean and SD; their outcome does not matter. The control that completes the pair is marked `RULE_VIOLATION`; the one before it keeps its outcome, since it was checked before the pair existed, and the lab's QC procedure decides about the whole run. R-4s cannot be saved without a window. Across-level 2-2s uses the same window (A-40). | Lab director to give the window from how controls are run on each instrument (for example both levels loaded together), and to confirm in interface testing that each instrument sends both levels to the host. | Lab director, B-6 |
| A-39 | **Undoing a merge is recorded by two people too.** When medical records reverse a merge in the HMIS, or the lab recorded one in error, an administrator or approver proposes undoing it on the merged MRN's page with the HMIS or medical records reference and why; a different administrator or approver confirms with their password, or anyone allowed withdraws the proposal. The merge stays in the record, marked `UNDONE` with who proposed and who confirmed; the database refuses an undo confirmed by its proposer. Afterwards each MRN has its own history, cumulative report and delta checks again, and the same MRN can be merged again later. Patients merged into the merged MRN before it was merged stay with it. Delta flags already written on results while the merge stood are not re-checked, and results already sent are not affected (they went under the MRN their order carried, A-35). | Confirm with medical records how a reversed merge is communicated to the lab, and with the HMIS team whether it will appear in the feed (L-6). | Medical records, HMIS team |
| A-40 | **2-2s within the run uses the same run window.** Westgard's across-controls form of 2-2s: this control more than 2 SD from its mean and another control of the same test on the same instrument, checked within the range's run window (A-38), more than 2 SD on the same side, each against its own range's target mean and SD. The control that completes the pair is marked `RULE_VIOLATION`; the one before it keeps its outcome. It is a separate rule (`2-2s-run`) from 2-2s along one control's own series, so the lab can choose either or both, and like R-4s it cannot be saved without a window. | Lab director to say whether the lab's QC plan uses it, and to confirm in interface testing that each instrument sends both levels to the host. | Lab director, B-6 |
| A-41 | **Instrument downtime: results typed in from the printout.** When an analyser still measures but its link to the middleware is down, a technologist or approver types its results in on the Instrument down page (W-25), one specimen at a time, for tests the catalogue says an instrument performs. Each saved result records the instrument, the run or printout reference, why it was typed in, who ran the sample and who typed it; it is a `manual` version, keeps the instrument (so release still needs that instrument cleared for clinical use), and goes through the critical, delta and review checks like any result. Whatever the two-person setting, the person who typed it does not approve it: another person checks it against the printout. Empty fields stay on the page; one bad value saves nothing for that specimen. **Assumption:** the instrument's own QC for the run is checked on the instrument, not here; typed-in results are not autoverified. When the HMIS is down too, the order is registered as a downtime order first (A-15), and its results are held until it is reconciled. | The lab manager confirms the downtime SOP: who may type results in, whether the printout is kept (and where), and whether a second check is enough or each value must be read back. | Lab manager |
| A-42 | **Report letterhead set in the middleware.** An admin sets the letterhead printed on lab reports on Set-up → Report header (W-26): lab name, a second line, address, phone, email, accreditation wording, report title, a footer note and a logo. The logo is kept in the database (PNG or JPEG only, at most 256 KB, checked by its first bytes; SVG is refused because it can carry script), so it is backed up with the data, needs no file store, and is served only to signed-in users. Each change needs the admin's password and is audited with the values before and after (the logo by its SHA-256). Until the letterhead is saved, reports print the `LIMS_REPORT_HEADER` line. **Assumption:** one letterhead for the whole lab; a report printed earlier is not stored, so reprinting it later shows the letterhead in force then. Nothing here is checked against an accreditation body's wording rules. | The lab manager confirms the wording (especially the accreditation line and the footer note) and whether separate sites or departments need their own letterhead. | Lab manager |
| A-43 | **Send-outs to referral labs.** Tests the lab does not do go to a referral lab. Admins and approvers keep the referral labs (code, name, contacts, notes) and, for each, the tests it does with the agreed turnaround in hours and what to send (Set-up → Referral labs, W-27); a test can be sent only to a lab with an agreed turnaround for it, so every send-out has a target. A send-out records which tests of a received specimen went to which lab, when and by whom, the courier or waybill number, when the referral lab received it and its number for it, and the papers kept with it (receiving report, result report or other: PDF, PNG or JPEG by their first bytes, at most 5 MB, stored in the database and only downloaded, never shown inline). Values on the referral lab's report are typed in with its report number as manual results that name the lab and report; whatever the two-person setting, the typist does not approve them, and they go to the HMIS like any other approved result. A sent test leaves the worksheet and Instrument down pages; cancelling a send-out (with a reason, before any result) frees its tests. **Assumption:** the turnaround clock runs from dispatch here to the result being typed in, and a test is overdue once that exceeds the agreed hours; the referral lab's own times are not used. **Assumption:** the referral lab's results are not received electronically; there is no interface to any referral lab. | The lab manager confirms the referral labs, which tests go where and the agreed turnarounds (from the service agreements), whether the clock should start at collection instead of dispatch, how long the referral papers are kept, and whether typed-in referral results need anything more than a second person's check against the report. | Lab manager |
| A-44 | **Turnaround narrowing and lab indicators.** The turnaround report (A-22, W-12) can be narrowed by test, by instrument (the instrument the result came from; "manual entry" for results typed in) and by staff member, and grouped by test, instrument, priority, day or person. **Who** comes only from what the middleware records: "collected" is the user who labelled the specimen at collection (the collection time is the one entered at labelling, which is the phlebotomy step when the lab labels at the bedside or the draw station), "received" the user who received it, "entered" who made the first value (a person, or the middleware for an instrument), "approved" and "released" the first approve and release events. Two more stages are reported: specimen to report (collected to released) and order to report (ordered to released). Other indicators for the same days and priority: results released, released within target (A-22), unreleased results overdue now, specimens rejected (with the most frequent reasons, by the text before the first colon), results autoverified, results corrected after approval, critical results called and the time from the first critical value to the first recorded call, and send-outs sent, on time, late, overdue and waiting (A-43). | A-16 | The W-10 audit export is **JSON lines plus a manifest** (count, first and last sequence number, SHA-256 of the lines), with each event's `prev_hash` and `hash` so a contiguous range can be checked independently. | A-19 | **Autoverification is off for every test** and ships with no rules. A test with a delta rule (A-20) is held when its delta check flags; a test without one is not compared with earlier results. Only the first instrument value of a new result is considered. It passes when every check passes: first value, instrument cleared for clinical use, plain number, units equal, inside the range, no abnormal flags, no instrument flags, not edited on the instrument, not critical (including units that differ from a critical limit), no delta flag. Every decision is stored with all its checks. | Require the QC window and a delta rule before letting autoverified results release without a person; decide with the lab director whether the HMIS should see "autoverified" in the submission once its format is known. | Lab director, D-1 to D-6 |
| A-20 | **Delta rules come only from the lab**, one per test: a largest change in the rule's units, as a percentage of the previous value, or both (either crossed flags), and a window in days. The previous value is the selected version of the same patient's most recent **approved** result for the same local test code (approved, queued, sent, confirmed, failed delivery or corrected), collected inside the window before this specimen; results still waiting for review are not a baseline. Only plain numbers are compared; units must match exactly or the version is marked "not compared". The flag (`EXCEEDED`, `UNITS_DIFFER`) is set once when a version is written and does not block approval; autoverification holds any flagged value. Patients are matched by the HMIS patient record only, so a provisional downtime patient has no history until reconciled. | Make an exceeded delta block approval, or ask for a reason, if the lab's policy wants it. Add qualitative deltas (for example blood group) once the lab names them. | Lab director |
| A-21 | **QC ranges come only from the lab**: one acceptable range (inclusive) per instrument, test and control, the control named by lot, name, or both, exactly as the instrument reports them (e 411 C-record or "QC additional information" setting, Mindray PID-3 lot, the QC sample ID for generic profiles). Each QC result is checked once at intake and kept with its outcome (`IN_RANGE`, `OUT_OF_RANGE`, `RULE_VIOLATION`, `UNITS_DIFFER`, `NO_RANGE`, `NOT_NUMERIC`); QC results without a range are kept but not judged. A control's warning stays on the dashboard until a later result for it passes. Westgard rules are optional per range (A-23); each range has a Levey-Jennings chart (W-15). Nothing stops patient results when QC fails: the lab's QC procedure decides that, except that an autoverification rule with a QC window holds results. Whether each installed instrument sends QC results to the host at all, and how it names the control, is to be confirmed in interface testing. | Confirm with the lab director whether QC review happens here or on the instruments. | Lab director, B-6, B-9, B-3 to B-5 |
| A-22 | **Turnaround times** come only from times the middleware records: order time (from the HMIS), collection and receipt times on the specimen (as entered at collection and receipt), the first stored value, and the first approve, release and accepted-delivery events. Results are grouped by the day of collection in the lab's time zone (A-24; order time when there is no collection time). Figures are the median and 90th percentile (nearest rank). Missing times leave a step out; backwards times are left out and counted. **Targets come only from the lab**, measured from receipt in the lab to release (the part the lab controls), one per test and priority with "every test" and "every priority" allowed; the most specific applies.
